# Patrick J. Kelly free-energy guide audit — Testatika-relevant material

## Source identification

The large internet compendium referred to in this audit is:

Patrick J. Kelly, *A Practical Guide to 'Free-Energy' Devices*.

A publicly indexed 2017 copy is Version 31.8, release date 15 June 2017, 3,318 pages. Kelly states in the preface that the work mainly reports what other people say and that he has not personally built and verified most devices described.

Evidence class for Testatika use: **S2/I1 lead source**, not a primary Testatika witness.

Repository rule: Kelly can reveal older names, diagrams, references and experiment ideas; every historical claim must be traced upstream before it changes the M2 baseline.

## What Kelly materially contributes

### 1. Source-discovery value

Kelly's guide acts as a large index into older electrostatic, pulsed, resonance, dielectric and experimental literature. Its greatest value here is not proof of free energy but identification of comparison systems and old source chains that may explain portions of the observed Testatika architecture conventionally.

### 2. Variable-capacitance / electrostatic-machine comparison

Kelly/Potter material groups Testatika with older variable-capacitance and electrostatic machines. This is compatible with the repository's existing conservative model:

`Q_i = sum_j C_ij(theta) * (V_i - V_j)`

and therefore motivates direct measurement of `C(theta)`, displacement current, phase-resolved electrode potential and torque.

This comparison does not establish net energy production.

### 3. Electret / dielectric pre-conditioning lead

Kelly reproduces or points toward electret-like interpretations of Plexiglas/insulator blocks. The historically safe consequence is not to claim that the original Testatika used a secret electret, but to add a controlled experimental variable:

- untreated PMMA;
- deliberately charged/pre-conditioned PMMA with documented safe laboratory procedure;
- discharged/neutralized control;
- matched material from different manufacturing orientation/cut directions;
- surface-potential map before and after a run;
- charge-decay/relaxation time versus humidity.

This is especially useful because the Testatika source corpus independently contains dry-air/humidity claims. A persistent dielectric charge state could alter field geometry, corona onset, leakage and startup behavior without implying an anomalous energy source.

### 4. Mesh/perforation as a physical variable

Kelly/Potter repeatedly emphasize mesh/perforated electrodes. The direct Holzherr/Baumann chain is stronger than Kelly and already reports that solid foil did not produce the same Principle Experiment effect. Kelly therefore adds no historical proof, but it expands the list of conventional mechanisms worth measuring:

- effective capacitance/fringing fields;
- corona onset and edge density;
- ion current/space charge;
- field penetration through the electrode;
- surface-potential redistribution on adjacent dielectric;
- leakage and charge relaxation.

The correct experiment remains geometry-matched mesh/perforated-sheet/foil A/B testing.

### 5. Electrostatic motor literature as control

The guide points to conventional electrostatic-motor literature, including Oleg Jefimenko and older parametric electrostatic machinery. These should be used as controls for self-rotation/torque observations:

- establish expected torque under an externally supplied bias;
- measure torque versus electrode angle and voltage;
- quantify stored electrostatic energy;
- separate motor action from generator action;
- measure load reaction.

### 6. Downstream impedance conversion / pulse-forming lead

Kelly/Potter suggest oscillatory networks, chokes, rectification and pulse-forming networks for larger machines. These are **not** justified as M2 original circuitry. However, they identify an important measurement question:

Can a high-voltage/low-charge electrostatic node be converted into a lower-voltage/higher-current output transient without apparent mystery while conserving energy?

The repository should therefore retain:

- complete `V(t)` and `I(t)` waveform capture;
- energy before/after storage capacitors;
- impulse response of any downstream network;
- comparison against ordinary capacitive charge sharing and resonant conversion.

### 7. Vladimir Utkin material

Kelly's update history shows repeated additions from Vladimir Utkin around 2012. Utkin's broader Tesla/free-energy material emphasizes feedback, resonance and unconventional energy interpretations. For Testatika research, this material is useful only as a **hypothesis lead** for feedback/phase-control experiments. It is not evidence that Testatika uses Tesla resonance or that any feedback loop produces excess energy.

Practical consequence:

- log rotor angle, node voltage, gate current and torque synchronously;
- identify whether a genuine regenerative charge-state loop exists;
- quantify every external bias/priming input;
- compare feedback enabled/disabled with identical initial stored energy.

## Claims explicitly not imported from Kelly

The following are not accepted as historical or physical facts merely because they appear in Kelly/Potter/Utkin material:

- ambient/zero-point/ether energy as the demonstrated source;
- electron cascade from the room as a proven energy source;
- plasma bridge as the known Testatika pickup mechanism;
- Tesla resonance as the original M2 mechanism;
- special crystal or rectifier identity inferred from photographs;
- pulse-forming networks as the known original output stage;
- over-unity conclusions based on high voltage, self-rotation or short load demonstrations.

## Best additions to the experimental program

1. PMMA pre-conditioning / neutralized-control experiment.
2. Surface-potential spatial map before startup, during operation if instrumentally possible, and after shutdown.
3. Charge-decay time versus RH and temperature.
4. Manufacturing-orientation/cut-direction control for transparent dielectric parts.
5. 15-rpm-region tests in addition to previously assumed 50/60-rpm regimes.
6. Four-terminal top-module carrier and full terminal permutation matrix at low energy.
7. Time-resolved energy accounting through any impedance-conversion network.
8. Instrumented rpm/load-reaction measurement rather than visual judgment.
9. Explicit negative controls for mesh-versus-foil and conditioned-versus-unconditioned dielectric.

## Bottom line

Kelly improves the repository primarily by broadening the **control experiment and comparison-machine framework**. He does not resolve the historical M2 circuit. The strongest new historical information in this research round comes instead from the archived Holzherr/Hartmann 1999 report; Kelly helps translate those clues into safer and more discriminating experiments.
