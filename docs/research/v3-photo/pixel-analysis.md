# Small machine V3 — pixel/video-level analysis with Marinov constraints

## 1. Goal

This document re-analyzes the **first small Testatika research target** using the high-resolution frontal image, the matching historical `meth4.asf` moving footage and Stefan Marinov's descriptions/correspondence. The purpose is not to pretend certainty where none exists, but to extract the maximum disciplined geometric information while separating source-supported constraints from reconstruction choices.

The moving source materially improves several external-geometry interpretations, but it still does not reveal the complete hidden circuit.

## 2. Source hierarchy used here

### 2.1 Primary geometric anchors

1. high-resolution frontal photo `testabig.jpg` / historical reference image;
2. `video/meth4.asf`, whose visible assembly matches the still component-for-component and whose embedded title is `Methernitha Testatika smaller 300 Watts machine`;
3. Marinov's statement that the relevant small disc is approximately **20 cm** in diameter;
4. Marinov's identification that the first small machine is the more complex small device and that horseshoe magnets are associated with that first-machine line;
5. Marinov's repeated warning that the exact **through-disc routing of the wires** is crucial and was not understood by him;
6. direct Marinov/Hauser correspondence scans from the uploaded historical archive.

`meth4` and `testabig.jpg` are treated as two media records of the **same visible assembly**, not as two independent machines. The `300 Watts` text is archive metadata, not a calibrated measurement.

### 2.2 Constraints strengthened by direct Marinov scans

The direct scans add several stronger small-machine constraints:

- one rotating disc;
- roughly 20–25 radial conductive elements in the relevant small-machine line;
- no rubbing collector brushes;
- Marinov says the described disk wires are **`connected to nothing`** — preferred baseline is electrically floating sectors;
- the side units are condensers with cylindrical grid + plastic insulation + central copper spiral;
- **two wires** are visibly routed to each side condenser in Marinov's referenced photograph;
- no conventional drive motor in the described small machine;
- Baumann used the term **`crystal`** in the Marinov conversation; Marinov specifically contrasts this with `rectifier` wording;
- Marinov explicitly rejects a Tesla-coil / AC interpretation of the small machine;
- he did not understand the full circuit.

### 2.3 Source-language correction

The previous repo text was too strong in saying no direct Marinov language reference had been found.

The primary scan `hauser/SMweb2.jpg` now shows Marinov writing that Baumann had **`ANOTHER language`** when trying to explain the principle.

The correct distinction is:

- **directly verified Marinov wording:** `ANOTHER language`;
- popular later wording **`like an unknown language`**: still not verified as an exact quotation.

Holzherr independently reports that Baumann was difficult to understand and used non-scientific language; Methernitha's own technical text uses non-standard terminology. These lines are complementary but not interchangeable.

### 2.4 What is deliberately not inferred from imagery alone

Photo/video cannot prove:

- exact polarities;
- hidden node connections;
- exact through-disc wire path;
- crystal material/I-V curve;
- whether dark surfaces are metal finish, shadow, dark plastic, backing or coating;
- whether every spring is electrical, mechanical or both;
- hidden thin interlayers inside transparent Plexiglas stacks.

## 3. Visual decomposition of the small machine

### 3.1 Base and rear board

**Observed:**
- reddish wooden base;
- large glossy black rear plate;
- visible subassemblies mounted in front of it.

**Interpretation:**
- wood is mainly structural;
- black rear board supports the geometry and may affect field boundary conditions;
- conductivity of the rear board remains unresolved.

### 3.2 Rotor disc and hub

**Observed:**
- one central transparent/translucent disc;
- radial fine routed lines/wires/sectors;
- pale central hub;
- two symmetric copper/brown-red arcuate features around the hub;
- a central vertical member in front of/through the rotor region.

**Moving-video upgrade:**

`meth4.asf` at roughly 00:00–00:07 shows changing highlights on the two hub arcs. The evidence now favours **raised physical arcuate pieces** rather than flat painted semicircular markings.

Best current geometric wording:

> two copper-coloured C-/arc-shaped physical-part candidates flank the central hub; exact cross-section, material, terminal connection and electrical function remain unknown.

**Interpretation:**
- rotor remains the main variable-coupling element;
- Marinov's direct `connected to nothing` statement means the preferred small-machine electrical baseline is individually floating routed wires, not a common ring;
- the hub arcs may be field-forming, balancing, mechanical or electrical elements and must be isolated as their own experimental variable;
- the vertical centre member may be support, screen or electrode carrier rather than a simple shaft cover.

### 3.3 Top `crystal` module

**Observed:**
- long horizontal red top unit;
- central light perforated/gridded section;
- darker end regions;
- two visible upright terminals/loops from frontal imagery.

**Source constraint:**
Marinov calls the unresolved element `crystal` and says the visible top bar does not reveal enough to reconstruct its interior. Holzherr separately remembers a different early small-model top assembly with four leads. Hauser separately describes crystals / possible rectifier functions on a larger machine.

**Interpretation:**
- external geometry may be modelled;
- internal component identity remains black-box;
- research carrier may provide up to four hidden terminals so alternate hypotheses can be tested without claiming any one is original.

Candidate functions remain:
- charge conditioning;
- threshold/one-way conduction;
- balancing/bleed;
- field-dependent conduction;
- isolated two-port coupling.

### 3.4 Left/right spring-loaded upper stations

**Observed:**
- mirrored small perforated supports near rotor;
- conspicuous helical springs rising to round cap/pulley-like tops;
- slanted perforated strips aimed toward rotor.

**Interpretation:**
- likely adjustable/compliant electrode assemblies;
- no evidence that the springs create rubbing rotor contact;
- slanted strips are strong stationary non-contact electrode candidates.

### 3.5 Slanted inner perforated strips

**Observed:**
- two mirrored diagonal perforated strips terminate close to rotor.

**Working roles:**
1. stationary induction/pickup electrodes;
2. field-shaping stators;
3. coupling electrodes separating charging and collecting regions.

### 3.6 Outer side panels — moving-video refinement

The frontal still already suggested a light coarse frame with a darker inset. `meth4` close-ups around roughly 00:40–00:47 strengthen this substantially.

**Observed layered structure:**
1. coarse light/white perforated carrier;
2. darker fine rectangular lattice/grid inset;
3. reddish-brown elongated conductor/frame/comb associated with a long edge of the inset;
4. separate visible conductor leads entering/leaving the assembly.

This means a single flat perforated plate is **not** a sufficient photo-faithful reconstruction.

**Unknown:**
- conductivity/material of dark inset;
- whether reddish element is wound conductor, bus, folded strip or structural frame;
- hidden interfaces behind the visible layers.

**V3 consequence:** geometry and material remain separate interchangeable variables, but the visible layering itself is now a stronger constraint.

### 3.7 Middle horizontal / side cross-panels near rotor axis

**Observed:**
- multiple perforated bars/blocks around mid-height;
- some extend from outer-panel region toward rotor;
- short perforated plates close to both rotor sides.

**Interpretation:**
- likely several electrode/node families rather than one monolithic plate;

### 3.8 Lower decorative/functional clover plates

**Observed:**

**Interpretation:**

### 3.9 Lower front collector panel

**Observed:**
- vertical light perforated front panel with dark inset;
- front posts/terminals below/near it;
- separate central vertical module behind/above it.

**Interpretation:**
- candidate output/balancing/central node family;

### 3.10 Lower central vertical module — moving-video refinement

The earlier V3 description treated the centre below the rotor too generically. `meth4` close-ups around roughly 00:50–01:05 favour:

- a **perforated cage/prismatic module**;
- visually distinct end/cap regions;
- separation from the lower front panel.

It is less consistent with a plain solid cylindrical post.

**Consequence:** photo-faithful V3 geometry should represent a perforated cage/prism while leaving hidden internals/electrical function unknown.

### 3.11 Lower springs

**Observed:**
- two long vertical springs end in separate lower eye/ring anchors.

**Hypotheses:**
1. compliant electrical links;
2. mechanical tension members;
3. deliberate long-path/leakage elements;
4. combined mechanical/electrical function.

### 3.12 Side pots — still + moving source + Marinov scan

**Observed/source-supported:**
- two large cylindrical side units;
- dense mesh/horizontal conductor texture;
- top/bottom metallic rings;
- dark conical/insulating-looking top terminal region in video;
- Marinov directly describes cylindrical grid + plastic + central copper spiral;
- Marinov states two leads are visible to each condenser.

**Interpretation:**
- strongest defensible role is a two-terminal electrostatic capacitor/reservoir module;
- video does not have enough resolution to decide which visible lines are outer grid versus inner spiral seen through transparent dielectric;
- large-machine Hauser three-grid/magnet/bifilar cylinder internals must not be imported into this small pot.

### 3.13 Lower central horseshoe magnets

**Observed/source-supported for the first-machine line:**
- U-shaped lower pieces are consistent with Marinov's horseshoe-magnet identification.

**Cross-source caution:**
A Marinov scan also describes another small machine without magnets. Magnet presence is therefore model-specific, not universal.

**V3:** retain first-machine magnet geometry but test real magnet vs dummy/absent.

## 5. Why are some regions black?

### Defensible observations
- repeated dark inset regions differ from light carriers;
- darkness may correspond to material, finish, backing, depth/cavity or exposure.

### Counter-evidence
Cathomen identifies at least one black machine material in workshop footage as black Plexiglas. This does not prove M2 dark insets are Plexiglas, but it strongly blocks colour-only carbon/graphite claims.

### Test matrix
1. bare metal mesh;
2. same mesh + insulating dark backing;
3. safely blackened conductive metal;
4. dark PMMA/backing control;
5. geometry-matched nonconductive dummy.

## 6. Marinov constraints applied to V3

### 6.1 No rubbing brushes
Gap-coupled stationary structures remain the baseline.

### 6.2 Rotor wires electrically floating
Direct correspondence now gives a stronger constraint than before: in the described small machine the disk wires are `connected to nothing`.

Therefore:
- no common hub bus;
- no neighbour resistor ring;
- no collector ring in M2 baseline;
- late 1-kΩ claims only as clearly secondary control rotors.

### 6.3 Through-disc route still unknown
Electrical isolation does **not** solve the geometric route. R0–R4 remain reversible route families; none is labelled original without new evidence.

### 6.4 Two-wire pot interface
Historical-facing research pots should provide two functional leads. Extra lab taps may be hidden/isolated for topology experiments.

### 6.5 No conventional drive motor in baseline
A lab motor is allowed only as removable test equipment for controlled rpm/torque sweeps.

### 6.6 Source-language caution
The correct source statement is now:

- Marinov directly wrote **`ANOTHER language`**;
- exact popular phrase `like an unknown language` is not verified;
- Marinov still lacked a complete schematic;
- Holzherr independently reports non-scientific/unclear terminology;
- Methernitha uses its own technical terms.

### 6.7 Crystal remains unresolved
Direct terminology is stronger; function is not. Do not collapse Marinov's `crystal`, Methernitha's `rectifying diode` and Hauser's large-machine rectifier interpretation into one proved part.

## 7. Working subsystem interpretation

A conservative experimental picture is:

1. floating routed rotor wires modulate electrostatic coupling as the disc rotates;
2. stationary left/right structures couple without rubbing;
3. layered outer/mid panels shape or collect fields/induced charge;
4. two-terminal side pots buffer/store charge states;
5. top `crystal` subsystem may provide nonlinear/asymmetric routing;
6. hub arcs and lower structures may provide additional field/balancing nodes;
7. none of this by itself establishes the source of claimed net output energy.

## 9. Bottom-line assessment after full video pass

### Stronger findings than before

- `testabig.jpg` is no longer a lone still: matching moving footage exists in `meth4.asf`;
- hub brown/red semicircles are better modelled as physical copper-coloured arcs;
- outer side panels are visibly layered;
- lower central geometry is more cage/prism-like than a plain cylinder;
- small-machine rotor wires have a direct primary-source floating constraint;
- side condensers have a direct two-wire-interface statement;
- Tesla/AC interpretations of the small pot spirals are directly opposed by Marinov.

### Still unresolved

1. exact through-disc routing;
2. exact M2/M3 mapping of every correspondence sentence;
3. crystal material/function;
4. exact outer-panel materials/connections;
5. hub-arc electrical role;
6. lower spring electrical role;
7. magnet function;
8. complete node grouping/hidden wiring;
9. historical energy source and any net-energy anomaly.

### Recommended experimental strategy

Use modular comparisons rather than one guessed circuit:

- E0 floating sectors vs explicitly secondary E1 resistor-ring control;
- R0–R4 routes;
- hub arcs conductive/floating/dummy/absent;
- bright/dark/insulated-backed grid controls;
- two-terminal pot internal variants;
- crystal open/short/R/C/diode/crystal variants;
- magnet present/absent;
- shield/environment controls;
- complete synchronous energy/torque accounting.
