## 2. The L.L. 17-March-1984 report contains a crucial source-attribution caveat

The witness report reproduced in Nieper's book does more than describe the demonstration machine. Near the end it says that the converter supplied current for a company/network through an **inverter and accumulator**, and that **additional current from two to three wind converters** was also fed into the same network.

This materially changes how a frequently repeated claim must be interpreted.

### Correct inference

The statement that a company/network was supplied cannot, from this source alone, establish the Testatika's isolated net contribution because the described system includes at least:

1. inverter;
2. accumulator/storage;
3. two to three wind converters;
4. the claimed converter.

Therefore:

`network/company power supplied` = **MIXED-SOURCE SYSTEM CLAIM**, not a standalone Testatika energy balance.

This does **not** prove that the specific lamp/heater demonstrations were powered by those auxiliaries; it only prevents the network statement from being used as isolated source attribution.

### Experimental consequence

Any modern replication or historical claim audit must separately account for:

- initial/final storage state;
- inverter losses/input;
- every auxiliary source;
- time-integrated Testatika input/output;
- load sharing between sources.

- https://dvr-raumenergie.de/verein/geschichte-der-dvr/

The page states:

- DVS founded in 1981;
- Hans A. Nieper was initiator/scientific leader in the founding context;
- from **1983** DVS issued `Mitglieder-Aussendungen`;
- under Nieper's presidency a total of **36 issues** appeared before the publication was renamed `DVS-Mitglieder-Journal` in 1998.

This is highly relevant because Nieper's book says DVS members inspected Testatika multiple times in 1983/84 and explicitly names Matthey and Nieper visits in October 1984.

- Matthey's original or summarized 20-Oct-1984 report;
- all five alleged DVS inspection dates;
- observer lists;
- correspondence with Swiss members;
- early machine descriptions before later Kelly/Nieper reuse.

No such content is assumed until the actual issues are obtained.

## 7. 1998 prototype report provides more detail but still no result

The Nov-1998 NEN item says Space Energy Association/Don Kelly was **building a prototype Swiss M-L converter** and describes Kelly's intended upgraded design as combined AC/DC for domestic requirements.

It also states Kelly estimated the then-design could supply about one third of a standard house's electrical requirements for DC heating/lighting.

This is a **design estimate/construction announcement**, not a measured result.

No later verified test report was found in this round. Therefore:

- prototype construction = historically reported;
- performance = NOT VERIFIED;
- success/failure = UNKNOWN;
- Kelly's domestic-power estimate = forecast, not measurement.

## 8. New-energy insiders explicitly acknowledged Testatika had not been independently replicated by 1997

Patrick Bailey and Hal Fox, writing in their own `New Energy News` field publication in June 1997, explicitly listed the Swiss Testatika among systems that had **not yet been repeated, demonstrated or replicated** in the commercialization sense.

Source:

- https://www.padrak.com/ine/NEN_5_2_1.html

This is unusually valuable because the statement comes from advocates/supporters of new-energy research rather than from an external critic.

### Correct use

It establishes a historical status claim:

`as of mid-1997, Bailey/Fox did not regard Testatika as independently repeated/replicated`.

It does not prove that historical Methernitha demonstrations were fraudulent, and it does not prove replication is impossible. It does show that strong Internet claims of established independent replication before 1997 require specific contrary evidence.
