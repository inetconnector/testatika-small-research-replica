# R4 rotor and mesh-vs-foil experiment add-on

## Scope

This document adds two **optional research branches** to the V2 baseline. Neither is asserted to be a verified feature of the Marinov M2 machine.

1. **R4 rotor routing** — derived from Hans Holzherr's report that sector wires on several Testatika machines were woven through the disc and changed sides three times.
2. **Mesh-vs-foil A/B electrode fixture** — derived from the reported Principle Experiment statement that a perforated/grid electrode worked while a solid metal foil did not.

The goal is falsification and controlled comparison, not confirmation of a preferred theory.

---

## R4 — three-side-change routing

### Evidence status

- Marinov: the exact way the early wires pass **through the disc** is important, but the route is not specified.
- Holzherr: on several machines, including a larger machine under construction, the sector wires were described as woven and as **changing sides three times**.
- Therefore R4 is a cross-source hypothesis with a strong reason to test, but it is **not** safe to label it as the known M2 original route.

### CAD implementation

Files generated by `cad/generate_v3_experiments.py`:

- `rotor_20wire_R4_3cross`
- `rotor_24wire_R4_3cross`
- `rotor_25wire_R4_3cross`

Each sector has through-holes at radii:

- 27 mm — inner anchor
- 40 mm — crossover 1
- 60 mm — crossover 2
- 80 mm — crossover 3
- 94 mm — outer anchor

Nominal continuous wire path:

`front 27→40 | rear 40→60 | front 60→80 | rear 80→94`

The CAD deliberately provides holes rather than a permanently moulded groove. Real wire can then be routed in R4 or re-routed without replacing the rotor.

### Required comparisons

Use the same rotor diameter, conductor diameter, material, electrode gap and bias for R0–R4. Measure at minimum:

- capacitance versus rotor angle `C(theta)`;
- surface potential;
- transferred charge/current per angular cycle;
- phase between rotor position and electrode current;
- electrostatic torque with a known external bias;
- effect of rear shield distance/grounding;
- humidity dependence.

## Mesh-vs-foil A/B fixture

### Evidence status

The Principle Experiment account attributes to Baumann the statement that perforated/grid metal was functionally important and that ordinary solid metal foil did not reproduce the effect. This is an eyewitness-derived claim, not a controlled published experiment.

Modern corona/electrostatic literature provides a conventional reason to test it: grid geometry can strongly alter field concentration, corona onset and current-density distribution.

### CAD assets

The generator creates:

- `electrode_ab_carrier`
- `electrode_ab_clamp`
- `electrode_material_template`
- `electrode_gap_gauge_1mm`
- `electrode_gap_gauge_2mm`
- `electrode_gap_gauge_3mm`

The same carrier and clamp must be used for both material conditions. Only the conductive insert changes.

### Insert conditions

Minimum A/B comparison:

- A — solid foil
- B — metal mesh/perforated sheet

Recommended extended matrix:

- solid aluminium foil;
- solid copper foil;
- square brass mesh;
- round perforated aluminium;
- multiple mesh pitches with identical outer dimensions.

### Controls

Keep constant:

- active aperture;
- carrier geometry;
- electrode-to-rotor gap;
- angular position;
- bias voltage and current limit;
- rotor routing/material;
- temperature and RH;
- measurement wiring;
- trial duration.

Randomize the order of A/B trials. If practical, blind the operator analysing the traces to the insert type.

### Measurements

At minimum:

- capacitance;
- leakage current;
- corona onset voltage;
- ion/current waveform;
- surface potential on the dielectric;
- torque or force;
- charge transferred per cycle.

A difference between mesh and foil would **not** by itself imply an energy anomaly. It would first establish a geometry-dependent electrostatic mechanism.

## Safety

These experiments are intended to start at low stored energy. Do not introduce an open mains-powered HV supply. Use enclosed, current-limited educational/laboratory electrostatic equipment and follow `docs/research/safety.md`.
